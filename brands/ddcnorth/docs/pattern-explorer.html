A pattern of possibilities.

The six-dot identity becomes an index, a field and a network. Complete six-dot groups carry the recognizable shape, while individual circles supply quieter textures. These supporting studies extend the palette and geometry already used by ddcnorth; they do not change the approved identifying artwork.

Additional kit studies. The identifying artwork and existing identity guidance are preserved.

Keep small text and the complete logo on a clear ground. Focal compositions are designed around one centre.

8 starting points

Choose a rhythm.

Explore a preset above, then change its scale, density or colour. Preset SVGs and PNGs are included in the kit.

01 / Repeating field

Micro index

Small, complete six-dot groups repeat on a tight registration grid. A restrained texture for the back of a card or a border.

02 / Repeating field

Turning groups

Three sizes and four quarter-turns share a modular field. The repeat changes direction while every six-dot group stays intact.

03 / Repeating field

Resolution

Individual circles grow and shrink through a repeating wave. A quiet field informed by data density, without implying measured values.

04 / Repeating field

Step repeat

Medium six-dot groups alternate orientation and rhythm. A bolder repeat for covers, wrapping and presentation dividers.

05 / Repeating field

Connection lanes

Six-dot groups meet along stepped paths. Solid junctions and a single bright route give a network field a clear rhythm.

06 / Focal composition

Convergence

Small and large six-dot groups gather around a central group. A focal composition for an invitation, chapter break or presentation.

07 / Repeating field

Circle weave

Three circle sizes interleave in diagonal runs. A repeating texture drawn from the identity’s simplest unit.

08 / Focal composition

Scale contrast

One large six-dot group balances a dense micro field. Use the open middle as a visual pause rather than a text container.

Editable applications

Put the pattern to work.

Give the artwork room.

Use small repeats as texture on backs, borders and large fields. Medium patterns work well in announcement panels; oversized motifs suit covers and displays. Keep long text on a clear ground and the complete identifying logo separate from the pattern.

Use one pattern family on a surface. Focal compositions work as a single field. Check a printed proof when producing small details.

Editable SVGs, outlined PNGs and PDFs, a reference sheet and a PowerPoint layout are included in the brand kit.
